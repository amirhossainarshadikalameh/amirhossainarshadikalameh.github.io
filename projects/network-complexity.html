Network Complexity Beyond the Degree Sequence

My B.Sc. thesis, Structural Complexity of Real and Model Networks, tests whether a popular way of scoring network complexity — the Shannon entropy of centrality distributions — measures structure at all, or simply restates the degree sequence.

The question

If a network is complex, its centralities should be distributed in ways a random graph cannot reproduce. Entropy of those distributions is often used as the score. But entropy is sensitive to how unequal the degrees are — so is it measuring organisation, or just heterogeneity?

Approach

  • Implemented 20 centrality measures — spectral, path- and flow-based, random-walk, communicability and information-theoretic — in Python and NetworkX.
  • Compared real networks from biology, economics and other domains against four ensembles: Erdős–Rényi, Barabási–Albert, Watts–Strogatz and the degree-preserving configuration model.

What I found

Entropy mainly reflects degree heterogeneity, not higher-order structure. ER graphs gave the highest entropy for all five networks in the comparison, and the degree-heterogeneous ensembles (BA and the configuration model) came much closer to the real values in four of the five.

That result redirected the project.

Current phase: centrality correlations

Instead of the entropy of each centrality on its own, the measure now looks at how centralities relate to one another.

  • Mapped the pairwise correlations among centrality measures across ER, BA and WS ensembles, identifying positive, negative and neutral correlation regimes.
  • Building a complexity measure from the mean, variance and entropy of those pairwise correlations — organisation the degree sequence on its own cannot explain.
  • Applied to enzyme networks and presented as a poster at the ICTP Spring College in the Physics of Complex Systems, Trieste, 2026.
